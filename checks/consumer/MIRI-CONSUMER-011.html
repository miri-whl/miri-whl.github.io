MIRI-CONSUMER-011 — Never reports `not-in-source` as proof a symbol does not exist

Never reports not-in-source as proof a symbol does not exist

must severity MEDIUM · 3 weight 7 Discovery Contract §3.6.1

Definition: standards/consumption/checks/MIRI-CONSUMER-011.yaml · view · raw

Violation
Client.request does not exist in this package. # resolve returned not-in-source; the package builds that surface at runtime
Compliant
Client.request is not statically defined in the source, so I could not verify it. It may still exist at runtime (dynamic attribute); treat this call as unverified.

Why this matters

Static parsing cannot see a surface built at runtime — __getattr__, setattr, metaclasses, re-exports resolved on import. A consumer that treats not-in-source as proof of absence is wrong about every dynamically-generated API, and would refuse correct calls against widely-used libraries. The evidence is deliberately asymmetric and the consumer must not collapse it.

Definition

A negative resolve result is weak evidence. This check verifies the consumer reports such a symbol as unverified rather than concluding it is absent.

When it fires

  • The consumer states that a symbol does not exist on the basis of a not-in-source result
  • The consumer refuses to emit a call solely because resolve returned not-in-source
  • The consumer treats module-unreadable as either existence or absence

How to fix it

Map not-in-source and module-unreadable to an 'unverified' outcome, distinct from 'does not exist'

  1. Map not-in-source and module-unreadable to an 'unverified' outcome, distinct from 'does not exist'
  2. Where emitting an unverified call, say so in the output rather than presenting it as checked
